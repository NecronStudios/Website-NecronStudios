VOID / DYNAMIC SYSTEMS

FEEL THE
WEIGHT.

VOID treats carried objects and equipment as physical elements of the world. The goal is not to manage abstract slots, but to make the player feel that the protagonist actually carries, manipulates, loses and recovers things.

TEN MECHANICS

CURRENT DESIGN / PUBLIC SUMMARY
01Inventory

Physical Backpack

At the beginning, the protagonist carries a real backpack with more than four spaces. To access it, the character removes it, places it on the ground and crouches.

  • Fully diegetic opening
  • About three seconds to open
  • About three seconds to close
  • The protagonist is immobile and vulnerable during the interaction
02Progression

Loss of the Backpack

At a certain point, the protagonist loses the backpack. From then on, storage is handled physically through the pockets of the trousers.

  • The post-backpack inventory is not a traditional inventory screen
  • The change is physical, not cosmetic
03Equipment

Four Physical Pockets

Four carry locations represent two rear pockets, one right-side pocket and one left-side pocket. These are physical locations rather than abstract interface slots.

  • Choose the pocket when storing an item
  • The protagonist performs an animation to place it there
04Storage

Shared Pocket Capacity

A pocket can contain multiple different objects at the same time. There is no rule that one slot equals one item.

  • Batteries, keys and other objects can share a pocket
  • Stacks remain physical objects
  • The limit depends on actual space and object properties
05Physics

Weight, Density & Volume

Objects have their own physical properties, including weight, density and volume. Their characteristics affect the load carried by the pockets.

  • Different objects can behave differently despite similar occupied space
  • Carried weight primarily affects fatigue time
  • Weight is not intended to drastically reduce maximum speed
06Risk

Dynamic Object Physics

Objects can move inside the pockets and, under certain conditions, escape. Movement increases the possibility but does not guarantee a loss.

  • Running, abrupt changes, jumps and falls can raise the risk
  • Risk depends on the individual object's properties
  • One object can fall while others remain
07Recovery

Dropped Objects Stay in the World

When an object falls, it remains physically present instead of disappearing immediately. The player can return and recover it.

  • A chase can become a choice between escape and recovery
  • A dangerous location may force the player to leave the item behind
08Optimization

Area / Time Persistence

Dropped objects use an internal persistence system based on area and time. The timer begins when the player leaves the relevant area.

  • Returning before the limit pauses and resets the timer according to the rule
  • Objects may be removed after the limit is reached outside the relevant area
  • The timer is internal and does not need to be shown to the player

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Do not manage
slots.

The equipment and inventory should exist in the world, not only inside the HUD. The protagonist has no weapons for fighting creatures. Threats are meant to push the player toward escape, hiding and decisions, while the equipment systems themselves can create vulnerability.

NOTE // Exact backpack capacity, weights, densities, volumes, drop probabilities, persistence times and other numeric values are not yet defined in the current design document.
